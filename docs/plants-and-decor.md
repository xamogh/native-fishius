# Plants and decorations

Current economy and lifecycle rules are in [v4 implementation, steps 1 to 3](workbook-implementation.md). That record supersedes older prices, purchase XP, care penalties, capacity and save compatibility statements below.

The source is the supplied `Aquarium_Game_Master_With_Plants_and_Decor.xlsx`, copied byte for byte to `design/aquarium_game_master_model.xlsx`. Its Decor Catalog, Decor Art Briefs, Decor Guide and Decor Live Ops sheets control the implementation. The existing fish catalog and XP curve are unchanged.

The source catalog has 120 items: 60 plants and 60 decorations. It contains 64 coin items, 32 premium items, and 24 limited items arranged in 12 coin/pearl event pairs. The current Shop shows launch items with available artwork in a scrolling row, ordered by unlock level with coin and pearl items together. Its All and Unlocked filters use the player's level. Each card keeps the source item's ID, price and currency.

## Purchases and ownership

Selecting a plant or decoration in Shop opens a temporary preview at the centre-front of the tank. It does not charge currency, grant XP or create an owned copy. Tap or drag in the tank to set the item's bottom anchor. Grabbing the preview keeps that point under the pointer. Its footprint is clamped inside the tank. A strong gold and ivory outline with a dark outer edge identifies it. The whole tank stays sharp, with no placement blur or dimming. The preview stays at its actual depth. Overlapping decorations, fish and food in front become translucent so the selected item stays visible. Items behind it and elsewhere keep their normal opacity. There is no name or price banner during placement.

The green tick buys one copy through Session. The price and currency are shown in Shop. After confirmation, a floating receipt shows the actual coin or pearl cost using the same animation as fish purchases. XP appears only when the purchase grants a positive amount. Currency, level, event availability and collection limits are checked again before committing the purchase. A successful confirmation saves the item at the preview position, charges once and records ownership. The first purchase of an item type grants the price-based XP below. Repeated releases cannot buy another copy.

### Purchase XP

The 18 September 2026 user decision overrides the workbook's zero decor XP. Plants and decorations use the same rules:

| Currency | First-purchase reward | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coins | Price divided by 10, rounded to the nearest whole XP with halves rounded up; minimum 1 XP | 25 coins gives 3 XP; 70 gives 7; 305 gives 31; 1,000 gives 100 |
| Pearls | Price multiplied by 10 | 12 pearls gives 120 XP; 20 gives 200 |

Each catalog item ID earns this bonus once across all tanks. Additional copies, moving, stashing, restoring, resizing and flipping grant no XP. Stored copies and event reruns retain their ownership record. Existing saves keep their balances and ownership; there is no retroactive reward. An item owned before this change counts as already owned.

Shop shows `First buy: +N XP` before purchase and `Owned: no bonus XP` for an owned type. At level 40 it shows `Max level` for an unowned type. Near the cap, Shop and the purchase receipt show only the account XP that can still be earned. The full first-purchase amount adds to lifetime XP under the existing cap rules. Crossing a level pays its normal coin and pearl grant once. Payment, ownership, XP and level grants commit together; failed saves and retries cannot consume or duplicate a bonus. Cancelling an unpaid preview grants nothing. The older paid-queue command grants XP at payment, never again at placement or storage.

Buying one of every regular launch item gives at most 43,278 lifetime XP, about 5.8% of the 742,640 XP needed to reach level 40. This is a finite collection bonus; fish sales remain the repeatable progression source. The importer derives rewards from current prices and records the rule in `overrides.decor_purchase_xp`. Workbook source cells remain unchanged. Changing prices through a future content import changes unclaimed bonuses only.

The red cross, Escape, F or S discard the unpaid preview. Enter confirms it. The regular HUD is hidden during placement. The tick and cross use the same 64-unit square size and scaling as Food and Rehome, with an 8-unit gap between them. They follow the preview as a compact pair centered below it, or above it when there is too little room below. They move beside tall items only when neither vertical position fits inside the safe area. Invisible touch padding extends outward to retain at least 44-point targets without separating the icons or overlapping the two targets. Dragging a button does not activate it. An interrupted drag or release outside the tank leaves the preview at its previous position. Unpaid previews are not saved.

A currency shortage exits placement and opens the shared funds dialog. Other purchase errors leave the preview available. A save failure retains the preview for retry, shows a warning and leaves currency and ownership unchanged.

Tap a placed plant or decoration to select it with a strong outline around its shape. Selecting another item moves the outline. Repeated taps on overlapping items cycle from front to back, including fully covered copies, then return to the front. Cycling does not move or save an item. A selected rear item retains the next drag through translucent decorations in front. Selected items keep a drag target at least 44 screen points wide and high, even when perspective makes their artwork smaller. Tap empty water or press Escape to clear it. Fish receive taps when they overlap decorations. Opening a menu or choosing Food or Rehome also clears the selection. Selection does not change the save or charge currency.

Drag a selected item to edit its position. The point where you grabbed it stays under the pointer, and the footprint stays inside the tank. Releasing a valid drag saves the position of that same owned copy for free. The tick, Enter or a tap outside the item ends editing without another save or movement. Escape, F and S also leave completed moves in place. The red trash icon removes the item from the tank and returns it to inventory. It keeps its identity, size and flip, with no purchase receipt or currency change. An interrupted drag returns to the last completed position. If a drop cannot be saved, the item returns to its saved position and shows an error; another drag can retry.

The four-arrow button directly above Sell opens Layout mode. Its generated cream-and-gold tile matches the other tools and uses the same size as Sell. The editing view hides the regular HUD, and fish do not intercept decoration gestures. Tap or directly drag an item to edit it. Tap an overlap again to cycle through its copies; drag to keep moving the selected copy. Tapping a different item selects it directly. Small finger movements do not save accidental moves. A short hint explains overlap selection. A compact pair follows the item: remove to return it to inventory and tick to end editing, with the same scaled 8-unit gap as normal placement. Touch areas extend outward without overlapping. A compact red Exit button sits at the top center. Exit returns to normal play and keeps every completed drop. Escape clears the selection first, then leaves an idle editor.

The aquarium's Bag button opens a centered Inventory dialog with the same tabs, cards, artwork and styling as the Shop. It has Plants and Decorations tabs and an All filter. Stored copies of the same item share one card with a quantity badge such as ×3 and Place in the action area. Stacks include stored copies from all owned tanks. The footer counts individual copies. The mouse wheel and horizontal swipes scroll through item types without placing them. X, Escape or a tap outside the dialog closes it without interacting with the aquarium.

Choose Place on a stack to restore one copy to the active tank and select it for editing. This saves immediately and keeps the other copies stored. The item uses the same outline, sharp tank view, translucent occluders and nearby remove and tick controls as other owned items. Dragging saves on release; moving the pointer without a press leaves it in place. The tick, Enter, Done, Escape or an outside tap ends editing without moving the item or taking another copy. Remove returns the selected copy to inventory. The count beside Done shows the other copies still stored. Each copy keeps its saved size and flip. A full tank or a failed save leaves Inventory open with the copy still stored and a message. You can retry Place after a save failure.

Each saved copy retains its identity, tank, position, size, stored flag and flip flag. Controls for flipping and resizing owned copies remain unavailable. Pending paid decor from older saves is preserved by the domain but has no current placement control.

The lifetime ownership record survives storage, transfers, duplicate purchases, reloads and event reruns. Plants and decorations have no sale or conversion action and no fish-stat or capacity modifiers. Their progression benefit is the first-purchase XP bonus. See [egg placement](egg-placement.md) for the separate fish controls.

A tank's decor score sums the workbook scores for distinct item IDs currently placed there. Stored copies contribute nothing. Extra copies of an ID in the same tank contribute nothing. Scores are calculated from placement, not incremented as quest or purchase rewards. The Tank Stylist objective can be completed by placing, moving, resizing, flipping, or restoring an owned item without spending currency. Opening or cancelling a preview does not complete it; unchanged moves and unchanged sizes do not count as rearrangement.

The placement limit is 64 copies per tank, shared by plants and decorations and separate from fish slots. The importer records this user override in `overrides.decor_placed_limit`; the workbook's original 32-copy input remains source data. Previously saved copies are preserved when loading, even if an older save exceeds the placement limit. Further placements require room. The existing 500-copy save safety bound remains in place.

## Catalog access and compatibility

The regular launch catalog is visible from the start, including in existing saves that have not placed a plant. There is no first-plant requirement. Purchases follow each item's level and currency rules. The current Shop filters by launch status and available artwork. The domain also supports configured event dates and checks them when a purchase is made. The older full tutorial still contains deferred gift and reward steps.

CP-01 Pebble Moss Tuft costs 25 coins and grants 3 XP on first purchase. Other items can be bought first. Existing bonus claims, balances, fish and decorations are preserved; this change does not grant retroactive rewards.

The earlier seaweed, coral, shell, arch and chest IDs are not in the updated workbook. Existing copies keep their original identity and artwork. The domain retains their movement and storage actions, although the current UI has no controls for those actions. They are not resold in the new catalog and add no new workbook score. No catalog item is silently substituted for an older item.

## Event configuration

The workbook describes proposed windows and requires an active configured event. Its proposed dates are retained as source metadata. They are not automatically activated. Lunar New Year and the anniversary require yearly scheduling.

The runtime accepts an optional `assets/decor-events.json` containing an array of event records with `name`, `starts_at`, and `ends_at`. Timestamps are UTC Unix milliseconds. The start is inclusive and the end is exclusive. A live-ops service can supply this configuration. This local application does not add a new backend or invent an event calendar.

Only the 12 workbook event names are accepted. Configuration validation is atomic. Event configuration changes acquisition only; stored or placed event items and their animations remain usable after an event closes.

## Artwork and motion

Every item uses its ID to join its catalog row, art brief, sprite and icon. Generated masters live under `design/decor-art/masters`; runtime sprites and icons live under `assets/decor`. Prompt records are in `assets/decor/provenance`.

The hourglass and snowglobe have separate empty-glass animation plates. The pinwheel has a separate base and rotating blade layer with a fixed hub. The original catalog sprite remains the reduced-motion pose. The glass patches preserve the original silhouette and alpha; their source records and preparation script are retained.

Art retains its workbook dimensions and bottom-centre anchor. Plants and decorations can be placed within the full visible tank, including the foreground floor. Saved coordinates, x 0–1088 and y 0–635, refer to the complete background artwork. Rendering uses the background's uniform scale and crop for anchors, sprite dimensions, motion and selection glow, so the same saved arrangement stays attached to the same scenery on phones and tablets. A different screen shape can crop scenery and its attached objects at the edges; rendering does not rewrite the save or slide objects inward. New drops also fit the scaled footprint within the current visible crop. If an item is larger than that crop, it stays bottom-aligned and reachable. The domain still keeps placements within the complete scene, including moves, resizing, restoration and old-save validation. The Fishium perspective remains: depth is `pow(ny, 0.82) * (1 - 0.44 * pow(edge, 1.6))`, with `ny` clamped from y 153.6–504.32. Positions beyond that depth range retain the nearest depth endpoint without snapping their anchor. The base perspective scale spans 0.42–1.18. The shared 1.2 tank art multiplier makes plants and decorations 20 percent larger, for an effective scale of 0.504–1.416 before the saved manual size is applied. A screen-blended #5fc4dc silhouette provides haze up to 0.26 at the back. The initial preview remains at world (544, 441.1904), uses full opacity and shares the placed item's live depth calculation.

Saved plants, decorations and placement previews render from back to front by the vertical position of their bottom-centre anchor. An item whose base is higher appears behind an item whose base is lower, even when the difference is small. The anchor is the bottom of the image rectangle, including transparent padding. Horizontal position, artwork height, manual size and workbook layer labels do not affect this order. Saved identity breaks ties at equal base heights, so selecting, dropping or reloading a copy keeps its layer stable. Perspective sizing and haze continue to use the existing depth calculation separately. Decor hit-testing uses that same order in reverse. Fish, eggs and food normally render in front, and fish keep selection priority outside Layout mode. During selection and placement, only objects that overlap the selection from in front become translucent at 22 percent opacity. Their opacity returns immediately when they no longer cover the selection or editing ends. The placed item keeps the preview size immediately, with no placement pop or scale tween. Preview copies also use the future placed identity so animated poses stay continuous. Runtime plant/decor motion still uses the authored local geometry, grouped motion and restricted internal or bubble effects. Ordinary coin items remain static.

The depth tint and preview outline use opacity masks whose color channels match their alpha at every texture size. Tint colors also include their opacity before multiplication or addition. Transparent padding therefore leaves the tank unchanged, including when a larger window or Retina display selects the full-size texture. The source artwork is unchanged.

At most six placed items animate at once. At most two external bubble emitters are active, with a shared six-particle budget. The independent Reduced Motion switch in Settings uses the still sprite and disables decor animation and effects. Its value is saved and restored. Event availability never disables an owned item's motion.
